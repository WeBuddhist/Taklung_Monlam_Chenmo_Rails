# The Practice and Recitation of Usnisa-Sitatapatra: The King of Birds, Garuda, as a Steed for Traveling to the Realm of Sukhavati ^0

## The Practice and Recitation of Usnisa-Sitatapatra: The King of Birds, Garuda, as a Steed for Traveling to the Realm of Sukhavati ^37-0

Namo Śākyamunaye! ^37-1

The supreme consort associated with Amitāyus is the White-Clad Lady.
This goddess is her extremely wrathful manifestation.
Her purpose is to bring about rebirth in Sukhāvatī, the Land of Bliss.
In order to bind the Mamo protectors and their companions under sacred oath, ^37-2

This goddess manifested from the uṣṇīṣa of the Buddha, the Sage.
Her profound purpose is to turn back sorcery and harmful magic.
It was not spoken from the Sage’s mouth; rather, from his uṣṇīṣa,
The sounds of the sūtra and dhāraṇī resounded; therefore it is called “Uṣṇīṣa.” ^37-3

Although this teaching belongs to Kriyā Tantra,
It can be practiced in a manner resembling Highest Yoga Tantra.
Jetāri, Vajrāsana, and others,
And many great siddhas practiced it in this way, making it easy to put into practice. ^37-4

Beginning with Garab Dorje and Mañjuśrīmitra,
And transmitted to Vimalamitra—this is the Nyingma tradition;
Together with the treasure teaching of Guru TrakthungNakpo,
The Indian scriptures of the Ocean of Sādhanas and the Hundred Sādhanas, ^37-5

And works by Meaningful to behold, Wangchuk Dorje, and others—
The essential meaning of these authoritative Tibetan texts has been gathered together.
This practice is easy to perform and possesses great blessing.
For turning back illness, harmful spirits, sorcery, magical attacks, and ritual suppression, ^37-6

It is like a vajra diamond sword.
If you wish to journey to the Pure Land of Sukhāvatī,
It is like mounting Garuḍa, the king of birds, and riding there.
Therefore, make this a regular daily practice. ^37-7

Aḥ! All phenomena are emptiness, beyond speaking, thinking, and description.
A White Umbrella marked with OṂ completely transforms, And instantaneously, complete upon recollection,
I arise with the samaya being and wisdom being inseparable,
As the Great Vajra UṣṇīṣaReversing Goddess. ^37-8

Of her thousand heads, the two hundred faces in front are white and gracefully beautiful.
The two hundred faces on the right are yellow, terrifying, and laughing fiercely.
The two hundred faces behind are red, scolding and threatening.
The two hundred faces on the left are green, peaceful, and compassionate. ^37-9

The two hundred upper faces are blue, fierce, and wrathful.
Each head bears a vajra mark and has three eyes.
Her body is white, and she possesses countless millions of eyes.
Her eyes are opened wide and glance fiercely like flashes of lightning. ^37-10

With five hundred right hands and five hundred left hands
She raises blazing swords that accomplish whatever is intended.
With her five hundred right legs extended, she subdues every source of fear.
With her five hundred left legs bent, she subdues all harmful spirits. ^37-11

She wears seventy million Buddhas as her crown ornament.
Above the crown of her head, seven white umbrellas revolve, one above another.
Every hair of her body blazes with rays of primordial wisdom.
She wears divine garments and the ornaments of peaceful and wrathful deities. ^37-12

At her forehead is Vairocana, at whose heart is a white OṂ.
At her throat is Amitābha, at whose heart is a red ĀḤ.
Below her heart is Vajrasattva, at whose heart is a blue HŪṂ.
At the center of her heart, upon a moon disc, is a white OṂ, ^37-13

Surrounded by a mantra garland whose light clears away obscurations and increases power and strength.
Her upper teeth are ten wrathful male deities, and her lower teeth are ten wrathful female deities.
Her facial hair blazes like a mass of fire, and her tongue is sharp like a sword blade.
At her throat are the sun and moon, while her own body blazes like a mass of fire.
Visualize all harmful forces being completely scorched and burned to ashes. ^37-14

<small>Even if the visualization is not firmly established in the mind, simply meditating on the focal object is sufficient;
by meditation alone, the suppressions, entrustments, verbal suppressions and the like are released, as taught by Guru Khrag-'thung Nag-po. It is important to recite this aloud.</small> ^37-15

HŪṂ! Great Vajra Uṣṇīṣa Reversing Goddess, ^37-16

Great Lady possessing one thousand hands, ^37-17

Great Lady possessing one thousand heads, ^37-18

Great Lady possessing hundreds of thousands and millions of eyes, ^37-19

You bear the blazing sign of the indestructible vajra. ^37-20

Through the great and vast vajra, you rule over the mandala of the three realms. ^37-21

Please protect us completely—ourselves, our people, our possessions, and all those around us—throughout both day and night. ^37-22

TADYATHA OM ANALE ANALE. VISHADE VISHADE. VAIRE VAIRE. ^37-23

VAJRADHARI. BANDHA BANDHANI. VAJRAPANI HUNG HUNG PHAT PHAT SVAHA. ^37-24

HUNG DRUM BANDHA PHAT. MAMA RAKSHA RAKSHA SVAHA. ^37-25

All buried curses, bindings, and ties upon the body, speech, and mind of myself and those to be protected— open, open! Release, release! ^37-26

All buried curses, bindings, and restrictions: SARVA APANĀYA SVĀHĀ! ^37-27

Pacify all evil: ŚĀNTIṀ KURU YE SVĀHĀ! ^37-28

<small>Recite this section seven times as the best, five times as medium, or three times as the minimum. Following that, recite the Heart Mantra and Near-Heart Mantra as much as desired according to your practice capacity.</small> ^37-29

OM SARVA TATHAGATA USHNISHA HUNG PHAT. ^37-30

<small>The Heart Mantra:</small> HUNG MA MA HUNG NI SVA HA ^37-31

<small>The Near-Heart Mantra:</small> OM MA MA HUNG NI SVA HA OṀ MAMA HŪṀ NI SVĀHĀ ^37-32

Through the merit of practicing this meditation and recitation, ^37-33

As soon as I cast aside this impure body, ^37-34

May I be miraculously born in Sukhāvatī, the Land of Bliss. ^37-35

Immediately after being born there, may I swiftly traverse the ten bodhisattva bhūmis, ^37-36

And through emanations throughout the ten directions, may I accomplish the welfare of others. ^37-37

Then look directly, vividly, and nakedly at the mind of the one who is meditating. ^37-38

Finding nothing whatsoever to see, rest clearly and openly in that very state. ^37-39

In daily practice, even if you cannot clearly maintain all the visualizations, ^37-40

Recite the two short mantras during all your daily activities. ^37-41

Alas! Because of the weakness of the merit accumulated in previous lives, ^37-42

It is like receiving a written demand for a debt when one has never consumed what is claimed. ^37-43

Although one has done nothing, one’s name nevertheless appears in a document directing harmful magic against one. ^37-44

One may fall under the power of hostile magical rites performed by various Buddhist and Bön practitioners. ^37-45

One may constantly be tormented by many illnesses and adverse circumstances. ^37-46

Although one remembers death and wishes to practice the sacred Dharma, ^37-47

One may lack the good fortune to practice the sacred Dharma properly. ^37-48

There is no certainty when death will come, and there is no way to turn death away forever. ^37-49

After death, there is no certainty where we will go—alas! ^37-50

Whichever of the six realms we are born into, there is great suffering. ^37-51

If there is anyone who keeps these things in mind, ^37-52

Make this instruction your constant personal Dharma practice. ^37-53

In former times, Dharma King TrisongDetsen ^37-54

Invited more than one hundred learned scholars from India, Tibet, and surrounding lands. ^37-55

They translated the teachings that became the Kangyur and Tengyur into Tibetan. ^37-56

At that time, when a protective rite was required for the king to repel harmful magic, ^37-57

Nothing more profound than this was found within the Kangyur. ^37-58

Therefore, this practice was performed as the king’s protective rite for repelling harmful magic. ^37-59

The Indian non-Buddhist practitioners and the Chinese Hwashang, ^37-60

And all the Tibetan Bön practitioners, were unable to harm him. ^37-61

Later, the Mongol Oirat king ^37-62

Constantly maintained this as his personal daily practice. ^37-63

The text says that although he destroyed the Karma Kagyu establishment, harmful forces were unable to injure him. ^37-64

Therefore, it is said that there is nothing more profound than this for repelling harmful magic. ^37-65

Furthermore, the meaning taught in this dhāraṇī ^37-66

May be summarized very briefly in verse as follows: ^37-67

The blessings and merit of countless millions of Buddhas ^37-68

Are said to be obtained by oneself through this practice. ^37-69

The obscurations of the five deeds of immediate retribution and all other karmic obscurations ^37-70

Will, without exception, be purified and cleansed. ^37-71

One’s lifespan, merit, and strength will greatly increase. ^37-72

Whenever the time comes to die and depart from this life, ^37-73

It is taught that one will be born in the Pure Land of Sukhāvatī. ^37-74

In this life adverse circumstances are turned away; in the next, one attains a pure realm. ^37-75

One reaches the irreversible state, establishing one’s ultimate spiritual destiny, ^37-76

And comes close to attaining perfect Buddhahood. ^37-77

In order that such a practice might benefit both oneself and others, ^37-78

Rāga Asya brought together the scriptural sources and oral instructions. ^37-79

The one called Padma Tharchin committed them to writing. ^37-80

If this contains incorrect mantra pronunciation, contradictions, mistakes, or other faults, ^37-81

I confess them before all those endowed with the eye of primordial wisdom. ^37-82

Through this virtue, may all obstacles of all beings be completely pacified. ^37-83

In their next life, may they be born in the Pure Land of Great Bliss. ^37-84

Having been born there, may they accomplish immeasurable benefit for others. ^37-85

<small>The White Parasol Reversal is as follows:</small> ^37-86

BhagavatīSitātapatrā, and your hosts of deities, ^37-87

Through your unobstructed power and strength, ^37-88

And the force of your compassionate aspirations, ^37-89

May you fulfil these prayers of ours: ^37-90

Cause the teachings of Buddha to flourish, ^37-91

And all beings to gain happiness! ^37-92

For our patrons and those around us, ^37-93

May tragedy and disaster be averted! ^37-94

May all bad signs in dreams be dispelled! ^37-95

May all inauspicious omens and circumstances be averted! ^37-96

May the malevolence and harm of enemies and hostile forces be averted! ^37-97

May the eighty-four evil omens be averted! ^37-98

May the three hundred and sixty forms of daily suffering be averted! ^37-99

May the four hundred and four types of disease2 be averted! ^37-100

May the eighty thousand types of obstacle-making forces be averted! ^37-101

May the eight kinds of untimely death be averted! ^37-102

May initial misfortune be averted!3 ^37-103

May persistent misfortune be averted!4 ^37-104

May fatal conflict be averted! ^37-105

May misfortune arising from mistreatment of ancestral spirits be averted! ^37-106

May sickness among men and women be averted! ^37-107

May loss in our resources be averted! ^37-108

May calamities in the atmosphere be averted! ^37-109

May calamities on the earth be averted! ^37-110

May unfavourable signs in astrological squares and trigrams be averted! ^37-111

May astrological hindrances and recurrent calamity be averted! ^37-112

<small>This practice for averting obstacles through Sitātapatrā was composed by DrupchenLekyi Dorje.</small> ^37-113

May all adverse circumstances be turned away!” ^37-114

<small>With these words, clap your hands. Composed by the great accomplished master Karma Dorje.</small> ^37-115
