# Sengdongma — the Lion-Faced Dakini ^0

## Sengdongma — the Lion-Faced Dakini ^1-0

I take refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the supreme Sangha
until I attain enlightenment.
Through the merit I have gained by giving and other good deeds,
may I attain Buddhahood for the benefit of all beings. ^1-1

Om svabhava. ^1-2

Within the emptiness of all phenomena,
in the centre of a blazing mountain of fire,
upon a lotus and sun,
from Hūṃ I arise as a dakini: ^1-3

Lion-faced and black in colour,
holding a curved knife and a skull cup filled with blood,
and raising a khaṭvāṅga staff. I am adorned with a garland of heads and the five symbolic ornaments.
My three places are marked with Oṃ, Āḥ, and Hūṃ.
Light radiates out, invites the wisdom being, and dissolves her into me.
I receive empowerment, and Akṣobhya adorns the crown of my head. ^1-4

OM SHRI VAJRA DAKINI SAPARIVARA AGHAM <small>[onward]</small> SHABDA PRATICCHAYE SVAHA. ^1-5

Glorious Vajra Dakini,
sovereign of the dakinis,
whose body, speech, and mind possess the five wisdoms:
I bow to you and praise you, protector of beings. ^1-6

At the centre of my heart is a ten-petalled lotus.
Upon a sun disc at its centre is the syllable HŪṂ.
As the surrounding mantra garland revolves, light radiates out.
May illness, harmful spirits, and obstacles be pacified, and may adverse circumstances be turned away. ^1-7

AH KAH SAH MAH RAH CHAH SHAH DAH RAH SAH MAH RAH YAH PHATAH ^1-8

Wisdom dakini with the face of a lion: through the power of your deity, mantra, and meditative concentration, may everything harmful to us practitioners—to teachers and students, benefactors and those around us—be turned back. ^1-9

This includes hostile enemies, ^1-10

harmful obstacles, circumstances that interrupt our practice, ^1-11

sorcery and harmful rites directed at us from outside, and, in short, every inauspicious and harmful influence. ^1-12

May they be turned back. ^1-13

May they cease to exist. ^1-14

May they be pacified. ^1-15

May the hosts of blazing wrathful dakinis
subdue every enemy and obstacle.
May they reduce their bodies and speech to particles of ash
and guide their consciousnesses into the expanse of reality. ^1-16

May the eighty thousand kinds of obstructing forces be pacified.
May we be free from harmful and adverse circumstances.
May our wishes be fulfilled, and may everything be abundant.
Through this auspiciousness, may there be happiness and well-being here today. ^1-17

<small>It is said that this prayer was composed by Khamtrul Kunga Tenzin.</small> ^1-18
